Common Causes of Water Damage in Broward County

Broward County sits at an average elevation of just 9 feet above sea level, making it one of the most flood-prone metro areas in the United States. But flooding isn't the only threat.

Hurricane & Tropical Storm Damage

Broward County is in a direct hurricane path. Wind-driven rain pushes water through roof penetrations, window seals, and sliding door tracks. After major storms like Hurricane Irma, we saw water intrusion in homes that had never flooded before.

Aging Plumbing & Supply-Line Failures

Many Broward County homes were built in the 1960s through 1980s with copper or polybutylene piping that corrodes over time. A burst supply line behind a wall can release hundreds of gallons before anyone notices.

AC Condensate Line Backups

In South Florida's humid climate, air conditioners run 8–10 months per year. Clogged condensate drain lines are one of the most common — and most preventable — causes of indoor water damage.

Roof Leaks from Sun & Weather Exposure

The combination of intense UV, salt air, and heavy rain degrades roofing materials faster here than almost anywhere else in the country. Flat roofs and tile roofs are especially vulnerable to slow leaks that go unnoticed until mold has taken hold.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly can you get to my property?+

We have crews positioned across Broward County and maintain a 60-minute response time for emergencies. Most calls in Fort Lauderdale, Hollywood, and Pompano Beach are serviced even faster — typically 30 to 45 minutes during normal hours.

Do you work with my insurance company?+

Yes. We work with all major Florida homeowner's insurance carriers and bill them directly. We also document everything — moisture readings, photos, equipment logs — so your claim is supported from day one. You should file a claim before we arrive if possible, but we can guide you through that process. Contact us for a free assessment.

How long does water damage restoration take?+

It depends on the extent of the damage. A single-room water extraction and dry-out typically takes 3 to 5 days. Larger losses involving multiple rooms, mold, or structural damage can take 1 to 3 weeks. We give you an honest timeline upfront and keep you updated daily.

Will I need to leave my home during restoration?+

In most cases, no. We isolate the affected area with containment barriers and run commercial dehumidifiers and air scrubbers. If there's a sewage backup or extensive mold, we may recommend temporarily relocating — and your insurance policy may cover additional living expenses if that happens.

What's the difference between water damage restoration and mold remediation?+

Water damage restoration focuses on removing water, drying the structure, and repairing damage. Mold remediation specifically addresses mold growth — containment, removal, HEPA filtration, and treatment of affected materials. Often both are needed, since mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours of water exposure in Florida's humidity.

How much does water damage restoration cost?+

Costs vary widely based on the scope — a minor kitchen leak might cost $1,500 to $3,000, while a full-floor flood restoration can run $8,000 to $15,000 or more. We provide a written estimate before starting work, and most homeowner's insurance policies cover sudden and accidental water damage.

Are you licensed and certified?+

Yes. We hold a Florida State Contractor's License and are certified by the IICRC (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification) in water damage restoration, applied structural drying, and mold remediation.
