Why Water Damage Happens So Often in Fort Lauderdale

Fort Lauderdale's geography works against homeowners. The city sits at an average elevation of just 9 feet above sea level, with some neighborhoods — particularly along the Intracoastal Waterway and the canal-front streets of Las Olas Isles and Rio Vista — sitting even lower. The water table is so high that heavy rain can't drain fast enough, leading to standing water that seeps into foundations and ground-floor units.

The April 2023 rain event drove this home: over 25 inches of rain fell in roughly 12 hours, overwhelming the city's drainage infrastructure and flooding neighborhoods from Edgewood to Victoria Park. Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport shut down for two days. Hundreds of homes experienced water damage that ranged from damp carpets to complete ground-floor losses.

Beyond major storms, Fort Lauderdale homes face everyday water damage risks. Many residential buildings in the 33301, 33304, and 33308 zip codes were constructed in the 1960s and 1970s — their plumbing systems are now 50–60 years old. Corroded copper pipes, deteriorating cast-iron drain lines, and failed water heaters are responsible for a significant share of the calls we receive.

Air conditioning also plays a role. In Fort Lauderdale's climate, AC systems run nearly year-round, and a clogged condensate drain line can send water cascading into ceilings and walls before anyone notices. It's one of the most common — and most preventable — causes of water damage we see in Fort Lauderdale homes.

Common Water Damage Causes in Fort Lauderdale

What to Do When You Discover Water Damage

If you find water damage in your Fort Lauderdale home, here's what to do right away:

1

Stop the water source if you can.

Turn off the supply valve for a burst pipe, or shut off the main water supply if you're not sure where the leak is coming from. Don't touch anything electrical if there's standing water.

2

Call your insurance company.

Most Florida homeowner's policies cover sudden and accidental water damage. File the claim early — it creates a timeline that protects you if there's a dispute later.

3

Call a restoration company — not just a plumber.

A plumber fixes the pipe. A restoration company extracts the water, dries the structure, and prevents mold from taking hold. Both are important, but drying needs to start within hours to prevent secondary damage.

4

Document everything.

Take photos and video before anything gets moved or cleaned up. Write down what happened and when. This documentation is critical for your insurance claim.

5

Don't wait.

In Fort Lauderdale's heat and humidity, mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours. The faster water is extracted and drying equipment is placed, the less damage — and the lower the cost. Read what every Fort Lauderdale homeowner must know about water damage.

Fort Lauderdale Water Damage FAQ

How fast can you get to my Fort Lauderdale home?+

We maintain crews in the Fort Lauderdale area and typically arrive within 30 to 45 minutes for emergencies in zip codes 33301 through 33316. We respond 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including holidays.

Is water damage covered by homeowner's insurance in Florida?+

In most cases, yes — if the damage was sudden and accidental (like a burst pipe or appliance failure). Flood damage from rising water requires a separate flood insurance policy, typically through the NFIP or a private carrier. If you're in a FEMA flood zone, your mortgage lender likely requires flood insurance. We can help you understand what your policy covers.

How do you handle mold after water damage?+

Mold prevention starts with speed — the faster we extract water and start drying, the less chance mold has to establish. If mold is already present, we isolate the area with containment barriers, remove affected materials, and use HEPA air scrubbers to clear airborne spores. All mold remediation follows IICRC S520 standards.

Do I need to move out during restoration?+

Usually not. We set up containment and manage equipment to minimize disruption. In cases involving sewage contamination or extensive mold, we may recommend temporary relocation — your insurance may cover additional living expenses (ALE) in those situations.

What happens if I wait too long to address water damage?+

Waiting even 48 hours in Fort Lauderdale's climate can turn a $2,000 water extraction job into a $10,000+ mold remediation project. Drywall absorbs water rapidly, and once mold colonizes behind walls, the scope of work — and the cost — increases significantly.

Do you work with my insurance adjuster?+

Yes. We document everything to IICRC standards — moisture maps, daily drying logs, photo evidence — and communicate directly with your adjuster. We work with all major Florida carriers, including Citizens, Universal Property, Heritage, State Farm, and Allstate.
